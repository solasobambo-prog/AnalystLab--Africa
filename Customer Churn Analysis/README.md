## Overview

ABC Communications Ltd, a telecom provider, is losing a significant share of its customer base and wants to understand why. This project puts me in the role of a Junior Data Analyst at AnalystLab Africa Consulting, brought in to dig into the company's customer data, figure out what's driving churn, and turn that into recommendations the business can actually act on.

The analysis covers 7,043 customers and finds that **26.5% have already churned** — a number that's heavily concentrated in a few identifiable patterns rather than spread evenly across the base.

## Business Questions

1. What does the customer base look like?
2. Which segments have the highest churn?
3. Does contract type influence retention?
4. Does tenure affect loyalty?
5. Which services influence churn?
6. Which payment methods have higher churn?
7. What actions should management take?

## Key Insights

1. **Contract type is the strongest driver of churn.** Month-to-month customers churn at 42.7%, compared with 11.3% for one-year contracts and just 2.8% for two-year contracts.
2. **Fiber optic customers are a high-risk group.** They churn at 41.9%, more than double the rate of DSL customers (19.0%) and far above customers with no internet service (7.4%).
3. **Payment method has a strong impact on churn.** Electronic check users churn at 45.3%, versus only 15–17% for customers on automatic payments (bank transfer or credit card).
4. **New customers are the most likely to leave.** Almost half (47.4%) churn within their first 12 months, falling to just 9.5% for customers who've stayed 49–72 months.
5. **Higher monthly charges are linked to higher churn.** Churned customers pay an average of ₦74.4, versus ₦61.3 for retained customers.

## Business Risks

- Heavy reliance on month-to-month contracts, which carry the highest churn rate in the base.
- High churn in the fiber optic segment threatens the profitability of a premium service.
- Weak retention of new customers means high acquisition costs aren't converting into long-term value.

## Business Opportunities

- Convert month-to-month customers to longer contracts to lock in retention.
- Promote automatic payment methods, which are strongly associated with lower churn.
- Strengthen the first-year customer experience, where churn risk is highest.

## Recommendations

1. Migrate month-to-month customers to one- or two-year contracts using targeted incentives.
2. Investigate and improve the Fiber Optic experience, with a dedicated retention offer.
3. Encourage customers to switch from electronic check to automatic payment methods.
4. Build a structured first-year onboarding and early-engagement program.
5. Review pricing and value for higher-paying customers to protect against upgrade-driven churn.
